# Requirements traceability matrix

Maps every mandatory outcome and module from the master prompt to its
implementation, tests and documentation. Updated at each phase gate.

**Status key**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Not started | No implementation exists |
| In progress | Partial implementation, phase not yet closed |
| Implemented | Code complete with passing tests |
| Verified | Implemented plus security, accessibility and visual regression evidence |
| Deferred | Explicitly approved deferment, reason recorded |

## Source of authority

The authoritative specification named by the master prompt,
`Unified_Invoice_Automation_Web_IMAP_Requirements_v2.docx`, is not present in
this repository. The master prompt is therefore the top functional authority.
Requirement identifiers below are assigned locally with an `MP-` prefix and
cite the master prompt section they derive from. If the original document
arrives, these identifiers must be reconciled against its numbering before any
release sign-off.

| ID | Module | Phase | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| MP-M1 | IMAP intake and secure manual upload | 2 | Implemented. Upload is wrapped as RFC 822 so both share one scanning, storage and audit path. Verified end to end against a real scanner and object store by `upload.spec.ts`, including refusal of a re-upload of the same content under a different name |
| MP-M2 | Malware scan, quarantine, safe preview, immutable repository | 2 | Implemented. | MP-M3 | Classification, OCR, extraction, confidence, human review | 3 | Implemented. Deterministic reading in `application/extraction_rules.py` with evidence for every value, classification in `domain/classification.py`, model assistance behind `infrastructure/ai_gateway.py`, and the review workspace at `/documents`. A model may only propose a value that is then located in the document's own text; a proposal that cannot be found is discarded and counted. Scanned pages without a text layer are reported as needing manual entry rather than guessed at: no OCR engine is wired in, which is recorded as an open item below |
| MP-M4 | Estimate workflow and approval history | 4 | Implemented. An estimate is raised blank or from an accepted document, edited as a draft, sent for approval, and decided by whoever the amount routes it to. `application/estimates.py` owns the lifecycle and `application/approvals.py` works out the route from the amount, the currency and the cost centre. Every round is kept: withdrawing an estimate closes the round it was on rather than erasing it, so an approval given before the request changed is still on the record with the name of whoever gave it. Verified end to end across three concurrent signed-in people by `estimate-flow.spec.ts`, including the two claims that need more than one session — that a requester cannot approve their own request, and that a Manager cannot decide before the Unit Head has |
| MP-M5 | Invoice register, linkage, validation, receipt sign-off | 5 | Implemented. An invoice is recorded by finance, linked to the estimate and purchase order it draws on, and cannot leave draft until a named person outside finance has been asked to confirm the goods arrived. `domain/invoices.py` states every reason it cannot move as a problem against a field, and the browser renders that same list rather than a second copy of the rules. Two controls are enforced below the application: a duplicate invoice number from the same vendor by a partial unique index that deliberately permits duplicate *drafts*, so the person who spotted it can hold both records open, and an overrun against approved spend with a tolerance defaulting to zero. Declining is a first-class ending that stops the chasing and says who declined it and why. Verified across three concurrent sessions by `invoice-flow.spec.ts` |
| MP-M6 | Three-hour reminder and escalation | 5 | Implemented. `domain/reminders.py` is pure arithmetic over a clock, a calendar and a policy: the interval is three *working* hours, so it stops overnight, at weekends and on the public holidays in `business_calendar_days`, and a reminder falling due while the organisation is shut waits for the next opening rather than being pushed back on each poll. Escalation copies in the next person up every third reminder rather than redirecting the task, bounded in depth and in total count. Every held-back reminder records which suppression applied, because an unexplained silence is indistinguishable from a broken scheduler. Delivery goes through the transactional outbox in `application/outbox.py`, keyed so that running the sweep twice sends one email, with bounded retries and an audited event when it gives up |
| MP-M7 | Configurable approval matrix | 6 | Implemented. The matrix is data an administrator edits at `/settings/approval-matrix`, not a constant. A rule is never mutated: changing one retires it and writes a successor, so an approval given last quarter stays explicable by the rule that was live when it was given. `domain/matrix.py` validates a proposed change against the whole matrix rather than against itself alone, and the screen states the verdict — overlapping bands, gaps no amount can cross, levels nobody holds — before it lists the rules, because a matrix that routes nothing is the failure that looks most like working. `RoutePreview` answers the question an administrator actually has, which is what a real amount would do. Who holds each level is separate at `/settings/approvers`, and an unstaffed level is named on the page rather than omitted from it |
| MP-M8 | Purchase requisition and PO controls | 6 | Implemented. A purchase order has a life of its own rather than being a reference typed onto an invoice: raised, approved through the same matrix as everything else, drawn down by the invoices linked to it, and closed when it has been honoured. `domain/purchase_orders.py` keeps "approved" and "closed" as separate facts, so a closed order is still an approved commitment and reads as one. Remaining capacity is derived from the invoices against it rather than stored, and an invoice that would take it past its figure is refused with the shortfall named. The threshold above which an order is required moved out of configuration into `purchase_order_thresholds`, per currency, so it can be changed without a deployment |
| MP-M9 | Payment package aggregation and submission | 7 | Implemented. Approved invoices are gathered into one package, priced per currency, and sent once against the reference the bank returns. `domain/payment_packages.py` deliberately keeps the package off the workflow state machine — open, submitted and abandoned are read from two timestamps, exactly as a purchase order's closure is — because the machine has no `draft -> ready_for_submission` edge and inventing one so a container could carry a status would have given every invoice a route from draft to the bank that skips approval. The invoices do move on the machine: joining a package takes one to `ready_for_submission`, leaving returns it, and submission takes all of them to `submitted`, which is also what stops the same bill entering two packages. The manifest is a deterministic CSV, hashed at the moment of submission inside the transaction that moves the invoices, so a file rebuilt later can be checked against what actually went. Assembling and sending are separate permissions on purpose. Verified by `test_payment_packages.py`, `test_payment_packages_api.py` and, in the browser, by `payment-flow.spec.ts`, which assembles a package holding one JMD invoice and one USD invoice, checks the two totals are never added together, sends it, and then finds every way of changing it gone |
| MP-M10 | Vendor payment check-in | 8 | Implemented. After a package is submitted, asking the suppliers is a deliberate second act rather than an automatic side effect of sending: `application/vendor_check_ins.py` raises one check-in per supplier, emails a signed link that opens outside the application shell, and records whatever they answer as a claim. Correlated SMTP replies are routed the same way through `domain/mail_correlation.py` and the intake branch that recognises them, so a supplier who replies rather than clicks still ends up on the check-in rather than in the document queue. Automatic replies are suppressed rather than chased. Paid and Outstanding are conclusions a named person holding `payment:mark_paid` records; the person who held `payment:submit` on that package cannot conclude it, and a supplier's "yes" never turns an invoice green on its own. [ADR 9](../adr/0009-a-vendor-link-records-a-claim.md) is why the link needs no session. Verified by `test_vendor_confirmation.py`, `test_vendor_check_ins_api.py` and `vendor-confirmation-flow.spec.ts`, which walks a real emailed link for one supplier and a telephoned claim for the other |
| MP-M11 | Credit-card statement parsing and reconciliation | 9 | Implemented. A statement CSV imports into a period of transactions; coding writes GL and cost centre (or an exception); receipt match or waiver lives on the line; closing freezes the period. Segregation of duties keeps the person who coded from closing. Assistive suggestions are stored beside applied values and never close anything. Verified by `test_credit_cards.py`, `test_credit_cards_api.py`, `credit-card-vocabulary.test.ts` and `credit-card-flow.spec.ts`, which imports a statement as one finance person, codes and waives as that person, and closes as another |

Visual baselines cover the empty register and the open → proposed → finalized → locked flow. Candidate and reversal rules remain provisional — see the open item | `test_accruals.py`, `test_accruals_api.py`, `accrual-vocabulary.test.ts`, `accruals.spec.ts`, `accrual-flow.spec.ts` |
